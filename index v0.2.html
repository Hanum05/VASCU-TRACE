07 / Research framework

About VASCU-TRACE

A proposed research direction for combining optical pulse sensing and electrical tissue characterization in a primary-care screening workflow.

Research background

Central aortic pressure and arterial properties are relevant to cardiovascular hemodynamics. Conventional central pressure assessment relies on validated measurement approaches; cuffless approaches remain an active area of investigation and require careful calibration and validation.

Research objectives

Explore whether features derived from dual-mode Optical PPG and BIS can be integrated into a reproducible signal-processing pipeline for conceptual vascular profiling and preliminary triage research.

Technological innovation

Proposed sensor fusion combines pulse-related optical information with frequency-dependent bioelectrical response. The research contribution is a testable integration concept, not an established clinical capability.

Expected contribution

  • Prototype architecture for synchronized dual-mode signal acquisition.
  • Exploratory feature extraction and multimodal visualization.
  • Transparent demonstration of a primary-care screening workflow.
  • Research questions for future calibration and clinical validation.

Limitations & future development

Signal quality, motion artifacts, skin and tissue variability, sensor placement, calibration drift, reference-method selection, population diversity, and clinical endpoints all require investigation.

Future work should include bench testing, repeatability studies, comparison with validated reference devices, ethics approval, and appropriately designed clinical validation.

Start in the Sensor Laboratory to run the synthetic PPG and BIS signals. Adjust heart rate and waveform amplitude in Aortic Profiling, then explore the rule-based triage scenario and research analytics.

  1. Use the anatomy explorer to select a vessel.
